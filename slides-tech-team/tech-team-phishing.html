Technology Team — Phishing Evolution

PHISHING NOW LOOKS LIKE YOUR OWN TOOLS

Same trick, sharper disguise — now styled as the tools and tickets you already trust, and arriving by more than just email.

Fake Escalation Ticket Risk

A ticket number and internal jargon are not proof — a fake escalation styled exactly like your own ticketing system, asking for a reset or a credential.

Fake PR Comment / Review Request Risk

A comment on a real PR, or a fake review request, linking to a credential-harvesting page styled like GitHub/GitLab's own login.

Vendor Impersonation Call Risk

A confident caller claiming to be a known vendor, referencing a real contract or system, asking for a reset, an install, or remote access.

Fake CI/CD Alert Risk

A build-failure or deploy-approval email styled exactly like your CI system, asking you to “re-authenticate” through a link.

This connects directly to this month's activities: the fake-escalation-ticket, vendor-impersonation, PR-comment, and CI/CD-phishing scenarios in Fault Finding and Decision Room are all built on exactly this pattern.
Starting today: Check the real queue or open the real site yourself, never via a link Your CI system and ticketing system authenticate through your own session, not by email Verify vendor calls on your own approved contact list
[VERIFY] GOLDEN RULE: A ticket number, a caller who knows the jargon, or a familiar-looking tool is not proof — verify through a channel you already trust, every time, whatever channel it arrives on.
