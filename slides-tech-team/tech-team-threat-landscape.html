THE THREATS THAT TARGET YOUR SYSTEMS

The same four forces are reshaping attacks everywhere — here's what each one looks like across IT and Engineering.

Ransomware via Endpoint or CI/CD Risk

A single unpatched endpoint, exposed remote-access port, or compromised deploy credential is often the entire foothold — attackers don't need much once one of these already has standing access.

Social Engineering via Help Desk or Dev Channels Risk

A confident caller, a cloned voice, a fake escalation ticket, a fake PR comment or CI alert — IT and Engineering are targeted because a reset, an override, or a merge is exactly what attackers need.

Privileged Access & Secrets Sprawl Risk

Standing admin rights, a leaked API key, an overscoped service account, a shared credential — most incidents start as convenience or an honest mistake, not a targeted breach.

Cloud, Endpoint & Supply-Chain Risk Risk

A misconfigured firewall rule, an unpatched endpoint, a malicious package, a poisoned vendor update — usually a mistake or a compromised third party, and it can sit unnoticed for months.

Most misconfigurations, supply-chain compromises, and vendor-update issues are found months after the fact — anything that behaves oddly right after a patch, package update, or deploy is worth reporting immediately.
Starting today: Hardware MFA + least privilege on every privileged account and CI/CD credential Patch on a schedule and check dependencies before merging Report anything odd right after a patch, update, or deploy
[REPORT] GOLDEN RULE: You can't audit every vendor update or every dependency's code — but if a system or build behaves oddly right after one installs, treat it as suspicious and report it. Most compromises like this are found months later, which is exactly why reporting early limits the damage.
