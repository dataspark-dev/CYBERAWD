Technology Team — Human Attack Surface

PHISHING & VERIFICATION PROTOCOLS

The technical controls in this playbook only work if the human verification habits around them hold too.

Urgency Red Flags Risk

High-pressure requests for money, wire transfers, or immediate credential changes are the oldest trick in the book — urgency is the tell, not the request itself.

MFA Fatigue Attacks Risk

Spamming push notifications is designed to force an accidental login confirmation — a flood of prompts you didn't trigger is an attack, not a glitch.

Out-of-Band Verification Good Practice

Never share secrets over chat. Verify executive or vendor requests via a second, trusted channel — never one the requester supplies.

Starting today: Tap Deny, not Approve, on any unexpected MFA prompt Verify unusual requests through a channel you already trust Treat urgency itself as the warning sign
[VERIFY] GOLDEN RULE: A request that pressures you to skip verification is the actual attack, regardless of how convincing the story around it sounds.
