DEEPFAKES: WHO'S TARGETED

The same deepfake trick works on more than a finance call — here's who else attackers are cloning, and why it works.

Fake Recruiter Interview

A polished "hiring manager" asks for a deposit or bank details to "process relocation."

Verify through official channels, never a link in the invite.

Vendor Bank-Change Call

A long-standing supplier calls — voice matches — to confirm a new bank account.

Confirm bank changes on the number already on file, not the one in the call.

Fake Client Renewal Call

A cloned voice matching a client's exec asks for an emergency discount or a redirected invoice, "before the renewal deadline."

Confirm any pricing or invoice change through your official account process, not the call.

IT Support "Callback"

Someone sounding like IT asks you to read back your MFA code to "fix a login issue."

Real IT never needs your MFA code.

Same rule, every role: Use official channels, never a link Check bank changes on file Confirm through your official process Never share codes
[VERIFY — EVERY ROLE] GOLDEN RULE: If a call asks you to move money, share login details, or break a normal process — hang up and confirm on a separate, trusted number before you act. Office or remote, it's not just finance. Attackers count on a familiar face and voice — the same trick works on any role because it targets urgency and trust, not technology.
