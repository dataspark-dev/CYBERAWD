THE THREAT YOU DON'T CONTROL

You can secure your own devices, but you can't control a vendor's security. See how one compromised update can affect us.

1 · Vendor Software Update

A software vendor we trust — fleet management, payroll, or ship management systems — sends out a routine update to thousands of customers, including us.

2 · Malicious Code Inserted

Attackers have already broken into the vendor's build pipelineThe internal process a company uses to build and ship its software updates. and hidden malicious code inside that update.

3 · Installed Automatically

The tampered update installs through our usual update process – no phishing email required.

4 · Backdoor Inside Our Network

Attackers now have a way into our network, all through a vendor we already trusted.

Did You Know?
Most supply‑chain breaches are discovered months after the update first went out – that's why they cause so much damage.
[REPORT] GOLDEN RULE: You can't check every vendor's code — but if something behaves oddly right after an update, treat it as suspicious and report it.
You can't inspect a vendor's internal code or process. But odd behaviour right after an update is still a visible, reportable signal – and reporting early limits the damage.
