Fake Recruiter Interview

A candidate is interviewed by a polished "hiring manager" who later asks for a deposit or bank details to "process relocation."

AI‑generated recruiters have been used to steal personal and banking data. HR should always verify any recruiter or candidate call through official channels — never via a link in the interview invite.

Vendor Bank‑Change Call

A long‑standing supplier calls — voice matches perfectly — to confirm a new bank account before the next invoice payment.

One recorded call is enough to clone a vendor's voice. Finance and Accounts should always confirm bank‑detail changes through a second, independent channel — the number already on file, not the one they just gave you.

Crew Allotment Change Call

Someone sounding exactly like a seafarer (or a family member) calls crewing or payroll asking to urgently change the bank details for a monthly wage allotment.

Allotment fraud is a known route for stealing seafarer wages. Never action a bank-detail change from one call alone — verify directly with the seafarer through a second, independent channel.

IT Support "Callback"

Someone who sounds exactly like your usual IT contact calls and asks you to read back a one‑time MFAA second proof of identity beyond your password — a code, app approval, or key. code to "fix a login issue."

Real IT support never needs your MFA code. This trick is growing because it bypasses the login screen entirely.

Whoever you are, the rule stays the same: HR: verify recruiters through official channels Finance/Accounts: confirm bank changes on file, not by phone Crewing: verify allotment changes with the seafarer directly Never read back an MFA code
[VERIFY] GOLDEN RULE, RECAPPED: Money, credentials, or a process change — verify through a second, independent channel first. On ship or on shore, it's not just a finance problem.
