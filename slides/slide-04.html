DEEPFAKE ATTACKS: THE CONCEPT

A 10-second clip can clone a colleague's voice and face for a live call. This is senior-leader impersonation — here's how a real person becomes a live clone.

Real Person — 10s of audio/videoA real voice or face taken from an ordinary clip — a call, voicemail, or social post. Even noisy, low-quality audio is enough. Mind what you post and record.
AI GenerationFree tools can clone a voice from 10–20 seconds of audio in minutes — no special skill needed. A few photos can do the same for video. Recognising the voice is no longer proof.
Live CloneThe clone can then run live on a call or video to mimic the real person. Attackers use it to authorise payments or request data. Always confirm via a second, trusted channel — never in the same call.

Voice Cloning

A short clip — voicemail, recorded call, or public video — can generate a live synthetic voice. It adapts tone in real time. Sounding stressed is not proof a call is genuine.

Video / Face Swap

Live face-swap can impersonate an executive with synced lip movements — as in the reported US$25M video-call fraud. A few seconds is enough to push an instruction. A second, trusted channel is the only certain test.

Synthetic Documents

AI can now forge IDs, screenshots, and supporting documents to make a fake recruiter or vendor look legitimate. Often paired with a deepfake call. Treat attached documents with the same caution as the request itself.

SENIOR-LEADER IMPERSONATIONWidely reported case, 2024: A finance employee at a multinational firm's Hong Kong office joined what looked like a normal video call with senior leaders and colleagues — and authorised a transfer of US$25M. Every person on that call, except the employee, was an AI‑generated fake.
The call included what appeared to be the CFO and several colleagues — all AI-generated. The employee only grew suspicious after the transfer had already gone through. This is why verification must happen before acting, not after.
