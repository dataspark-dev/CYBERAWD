WHAT NEVER GOES INTO A PUBLIC PROMPT

Hygiene Data Handling Endpoint Network AI Leakage Golden Rules

A public AI tool is a third party outside your data boundary — treat what you paste into it accordingly.

Secrets, Credentials, API Keys Risk

Never pasted into a public AI prompt, ever — a leaked key inside a chat log is functionally the same as one committed to a public repo.

PII And Incident Logs Risk

Customer or employee personal data, and details from an active security incident, both stay out of a public prompt — the same Confidential/Restricted handling from the data-classification tiers applies here too.

Token Metrics Are Fine To Log Good Practice

Usage volume, token counts, and cost/latency metrics are safe, useful telemetry — this is exactly what powers denial-of-wallet and abuse monitoring.

Raw Prompts/Responses Need Strict Exclusion Risk

Full prompt and response content is where secrets and PII actually leak — mandatory telemetry (metrics) and strict log exclusion (raw content) are two different policies, not one.

This connects directly to this month's activities: the same “never paste sensitive data into public AI” habit from this month's chatbot-risk material applies just as much to IT's own tickets, logs, and incident notes as to anyone else's work.
Starting today: Never paste secrets, PII, or incident details into a public AI prompt Token/cost metrics: safe and useful to log Raw prompt/response content: strict exclusion from logs
[AI DATA LEAKAGE] GOLDEN RULE: Split your AI telemetry in two — metrics you always log, and raw content you never do. What's Confidential or Restricted offline is exactly as Confidential or Restricted inside an AI prompt.
