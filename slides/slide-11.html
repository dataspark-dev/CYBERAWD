Everyday Defence

YOUR DESK IS PART OF THE PERIMETER

Four things worth noticing at any desk — two risks, two good habits. Plus what should happen the moment you walk away.

Password on a Sticky Note Risk

Never leave passwords written where others can see them. Use a password manager instead.

Locked Screen Good Practice

Lock your screen (Win+L / Cmd+Ctrl+Q) every time you leave, even for a moment.

Unknown USB Drive Risk

Never plug in a USB stick you don't own or haven't checked with IT. It can install harmful software silently the moment you plug it in.

Badge Worn Visibly Good Practice

Keep your badge visible, and don't let anyone follow you through a secure door.

What Should Happen When You Walk Away

After a short idle period, the screen should lock automatically — no action needed from you. If it doesn't, lock it yourself before you go.

Locking takes 2 seconds. Leaving it unlocked is an open invitation.
Same rule on board: bridge, ECDIS, and engine-control computers are just as exposed as a desk — lock them on watch handover.
Before you step away — office or onboard: Lock your screen (Win+L / Cmd+Ctrl+Q) Put away sticky notes & USB drives Wear your badge, don't tailgate others
[LOCK IT] GOLDEN RULE: If you can see it, so can anyone walking past – lock your screen, secure loose items, and never plug in an unknown drive.
Physical access bypasses almost every technical control. A locked screen and a secured desk close that gap in seconds – and it costs nothing.
