DEEPFAKES: WHO'S TARGETED

The same deepfake trick works on more than a finance call — here's who else attackers are cloning, and why it works.

Fake Recruiter Interview

A candidate is interviewed by a polished "hiring manager" who later asks for a deposit or bank details to "process relocation." AI-generated recruiters have stolen personal and banking data this way — HR should verify any recruiter call through official channels, never via a link in the invite.

Vendor Bank-Change Call

A long-standing supplier calls — voice matches — to confirm a new bank account before the next payment. One call can clone a vendor's voice. Always confirm bank changes on the number already on file.

Crew Allotment Change Call

Someone sounding like a seafarer calls crewing to urgently change bank details for a wage allotment. Allotment fraud steals seafarer wages — confirm any bank change directly with the seafarer on a known number.

IT Support "Callback"

Someone sounding like your IT contact asks you to read back your MFA code to "fix a login issue." Real IT never needs your MFA code — the trick bypasses the login screen entirely.

Same rule, every role: HR: use official channels Finance: check bank changes on file Crewing: confirm with the seafarer Never share codes
[VERIFY — EVERY ROLE] GOLDEN RULE: If a call asks you to move money, share login details, or break a normal process — hang up and confirm on a separate, trusted number before you act. Ship or shore, it's not just finance.
