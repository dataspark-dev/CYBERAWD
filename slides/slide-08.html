Email, SMS & Voice

PHISHING GOES MULTI-CHANNEL

Email isn't the only way in. Three real examples — and the tells that give each one away.

Smishing (SMS)
Text Message · Synergy Crew — +91 98xxx
Synergy Marine: Your Fleet Portal rotation link has changed. Confirm your MV Horizon sign-off dates here: bit.ly/CrewRotation-Horizon or your account will be delayed. — Fleet Ops
Short link hides the real address

Synergy never sends the crew portal as a bit.ly link by text.

Fake urgency

"Or your account will be delayed" — pressure to tap before you check.

Told not to check

A real Fleet Ops message would never say "use this link only — don't call."

Vishing (Voice)
"IT Service Desk — Fleet Portal"
Incoming Call — Ship Phone
"Hi, this is IT Support about your Fleet Portal — please confirm your password and the code from your phone so we can fix your rotation access."
Caller name can be faked

The display name can say "IT Service Desk" even when the number isn't Synergy's.

Asking for your password or code

Real IT never asks you to say your password or a code on a call they made to you.

"Stay on the line"

So you don't hang up and call back on a number you already trust.

Work Chat (Teams/WhatsApp)
Teams Chat · "Priya Sharma" (Finance)
Hi, this is Priya from Finance — can you approve this payment via the new link before the call? Don't mention it in the group chat yet.
Private message bypasses the team channel

Everyone else would see a request like this in the shared channel.

Urgency and secrecy together

A classic pressure tactic — legitimate requests don't need silence.

Across every channel: Don't tap links in unexpected texts Hang up, call a number you already have saved Use the visible team channel, not a DM
[STOP — EVERY CHANNEL] GOLDEN RULE: Same tells on every channel — urgency, faked identity, pressure to act. Hang up, close the chat, use a number you already have.
Attackers switch channels to lower your guard — but the tells stay the same.
