A CLONED VOICE ON A CODE-REVIEW CALL

A 10-second clip can clone a colleague's voice and face for a live call — increasingly aimed at engineering leadership approving a merge, deploy, or access grant, not just finance approving a wire.

Urgency to Merge or Deploy Now Risk

A cloned “engineering lead” pushing to bypass review and merge or deploy immediately is the exact pressure this attack is built to create.

Fake Emergency Access Request Risk

A synthetic voice or video call requesting emergency credentials or access “to unblock the release” — recognising the face or voice is no longer proof.

Verify Via a Second Channel Good Practice

Confirm any unusual request — a merge, a deploy, a credential — through a channel outside the call itself, before acting.

Pause Before Authorising Good Practice

A request that can't wait 5 minutes for verification is exactly the kind that most needs it.

Widely reported case, 2024: A finance employee authorised a US$25M transfer on a video call where every other participant was an AI-generated fake senior leader. The same tactic is increasingly aimed at engineering leadership approving a deploy or an access grant instead of a wire transfer.
Starting today: Verify via a 2nd channel, never in the same call Pause before merging, deploying, or sharing access Report suspicious video/voice calls
[VERIFY] GOLDEN RULE: Recognising a face or voice is not verification — confirm any request to merge, deploy, or share access through a separate, trusted channel, every time.
