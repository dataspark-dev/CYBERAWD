# Live Cybersecurity Awareness Games — Execution Plan

Companion event to the *Synergy Cyber Security Awareness Month* slide deck (see [slide-19](slides/slide-19.html), "Take It Further"). Turns four planned activities into a runnable session.

## 1. Overview

- **Objective**: reinforce the deck's core habits (spot it, verify it, report it) through hands-on, timed activities.
- **Format**: rotating stations — all 4 games run simultaneously in parallel; teams rotate through all of them.
- **Headcount assumption**: ~24 participants (adjust team sizes proportionally for other counts up to ~30).
- **Total runtime budget**: ~40 minutes (fits the 30–45 minute window).

## 3. Team & rotation matrix

Split into **4 teams (A–D)**. Each team visits all 4 stations, offset so no station is ever empty or doubled up:

| Rotation | Team A | Team B | Team C | Team D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Station 1 | Station 2 | Station 3 | Station 4 |
| 2 | Station 2 | Station 3 | Station 4 | Station 1 |
| 3 | Station 3 | Station 4 | Station 1 | Station 2 |
| 4 | Station 4 | Station 1 | Station 2 | Station 3 |

For headcounts other than ~24, keep 4 teams and scale team size (e.g., 5 teams if the group is larger than ~30 — but note this pushes past the "small" format and would need a longer runtime).

## 4. Station run-sheets

### Station 1 — Digital Platform: Cybersecurity Game (InfosecIQ)
- **Purpose**: reinforce judgment under a realistic, fast scenario.
- **Setup**: 1 tablet/laptop or shared screen per team, InfosecIQ link pre-loaded.
- **Flow**: play the ~30s animated scenario video → team answers the follow-up question together.
- **Facilitator script**: after the answer, spend ~1 minute on *why* the right answer is right — tie it back to the deck's STOP → VERIFY → REPORT rule.
- **Timing**: video (30s) + question (30s) + discussion (~1 min) + buffer → fits 7 min with 2–3 rounds if the team finishes early.
- **Scoring**: 1 point per correct answer.

### Station 2 — Digital Platform: Fault Finding
- **Purpose**: pattern-recognition practice across multiple attack types.
- **Setup**: TV/display screen, a queued set of scenario images (see §8), answer key on a facilitator card.
- **Flow**: display one scenario image at a time → team calls out what's wrong and why → facilitator reveals the answer key.
- **Timing**: ~1 min per image × 5 images ≈ 5–6 min, leaving buffer for discussion.
- **Scoring**: 1 point for correct identification, +1 bonus for a correct explanation of *why* it's suspicious.

### Station 3 — Cyber Clue Quest
- **Purpose**: team problem-solving under light time pressure.
- **Setup**: letter stencil sets (one per active sub-group), riddle cards (see §8), a visible 30-second timer.
- **Flow**: split the team into 2 sub-groups (for parallel play) → read a riddle aloud → sub-groups race to spell the answer with stencils within 30 seconds.
- **Timing**: ~1 min per riddle (read + 30s solve + reveal) × ~5 riddles ≈ 5–7 min.
- **Scoring**: first correct sub-group per riddle earns the point; unsolved riddles carry no penalty.

### Station 4 — Pass-Phrase
- **Purpose**: reinforce specific terminology (MFA, zero trust, phishing, etc.) through recall-under-pressure.
- **Setup**: pre-scrambled letter stencil sets, one scrambled phrase per round, 30-second timer.
- **Flow**: reveal a scrambled term → team races to arrange the stencils into the correct word/phrase within 30 seconds.
- **Timing**: ~1 min per phrase (reveal + 30s solve + reveal answer) × ~5 phrases ≈ 5–7 min.
- **Scoring**: 1 point per phrase solved within time; no partial credit for a wrong arrangement.

## 5. Staffing plan

- 1 facilitator per station × 4 stations = 4 people (owns their station's script, scoring, and timing).
- 1 floater/MC: keeps the overall rotation on schedule (whistle/bell at each transition), aggregates scores, runs the wrap-up.
- **Total: 5 staff.**

## 7. Scoring & prizes

- MC keeps a running scoreboard, tallying points from each station after every rotation.
- At wrap-up (min 36–41), MC reads out the leaderboard and awards the top team(s).
- Keep it light — the scoreboard is for engagement, not a formal assessment.

## 8. Ready-to-run content appendix

Sample content using terms already covered in the deck. Swap for InfosecIQ's own content pack if preferred.

**Cyber Clue Quest — sample riddles:**
1. "I trick you into clicking, dressed as a trusted friend. I'm the oldest trick that never seems to end." → **PHISHING**
2. "I lock your files and demand a fee. Pay or not, your data's held by me." → **RANSOMWARE**
3. "Two steps, not one, to prove it's really you. A password alone just won't do." → **MFA**
4. "I'm the wall that stands between you and the storm outside your screen." → **FIREWALL**
5. "Trust nothing by default, verify every request — that's the rule attackers dread the most." → **ZEROTRUST**
6. "A face, a voice — but neither one is real. I'm made by AI to help attackers steal." → **DEEPFAKE**

**Pass-Phrase — sample scrambled terms:**
1. `GNOSTRPASSWORD` → **STRONG PASSWORD**
2. `LAICOSGNIREENIGNE` → **SOCIAL ENGINEERING**
3. `OTFACROTUHTNOITAC` → **TWO FACTOR AUTH** *(present as "TWOFACTORAUTH" if spacing stencils are limited)*
4. `TROPERTIFICNEDI` → **REPORT INCIDENT**
5. `KCILCTONOD` → **DONT CLICK**

**Fault Finding — suggested scenario image set:**
1. A phishing email with a mismatched sender domain and urgency language.
2. A ransomware note / lock-screen mockup.
3. A smishing text asking to "verify" a delivery via a link.
4. A tailgating photo (someone holding a badge-access door for a stranger).
5. A fake IT support pop-up asking for a password.

## 9. Contingency notes

- **Station 1 tech failure** (InfosecIQ link/Wi-Fi down): facilitator plays the scenario from a personal phone/hotspot as backup, or substitutes a printed scenario card with the same discussion questions.
- **Running over time**: trim Station 1's post-answer discussion first — it's the only station without a hard 30-second mechanic, so it's the easiest to compress without breaking the game.
- **A station finishes early**: have 1–2 extra riddles/phrases/images in reserve per station so early finishers aren't left idle.
